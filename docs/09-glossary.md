# Glossary: The Words They Will Say on Day One

**Education only — not medical advice.** Definitions here are plain-English paraphrases for orientation survival, not technical or legal definitions, and never a substitute for your facility's policy definitions or your state's practice act.

New nurses get buried by vocabulary before they get buried by patients. This is the translation layer — the words people use casually in week one, what they actually mean, and what you do when you hear them.

## Handoff and team words

| Term | Plain meaning | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Report / handoff | The verbal handover of each patient at shift change | [Chapter 3](03-organizing-report-notes.html) organizes what you write down |
| SBAR | Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation — the standard way to structure a call or handoff | Ends with what you want ("I need you to assess") |
| Bedside report | Report happens in the room with the patient | Introduce yourself during it |
| Charge nurse | The shift's traffic controller, not (usually) the clinical lead | Your first escalation step, most questions |
| Preceptor | The nurse assigned to teach you for orientation | [Chapter 4](04-preceptors-and-feedback.html) |
| Resource nurse / float | Extra nurse not carrying an assignment, to help | A gift when offered; take it once with grace |
| Rapid response | A team that rapidly assesses a deteriorating patient, before a full code | Decide to call early; "I'm uncomfortable" is enough |
| Code blue | Cardiac arrest emergency response | Know where you go and your role before you need it |

## People and roles

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Attending / hospitalist / provider | The physicians and advanced-practice clinicians who write orders |
| Interdisciplinary rounds (IDR) | Once-daily team meeting on each patient's plan |
| Case management / social work | Discharge logistics and post-hospital placement people |
| PT / OT / SLP | Physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology |
| Techs / aides / PCT | Unlicensed team members who help with vitals, hygiene, turns |
| House supervisor | The building-wide nurse manager after hours |

## Paper and policy words

| Term | Plain meaning | What a first-year does |
|---|---|---|
| Code status | What resuscitation the patient wants (e.g., full code vs. limited) | Read it at report; never assume; escalate unclear cases |
| DNR / POLST | Do-not-resuscitate; portable medical order recording patient wishes | Policy rules; ask early if unsure |
| Advance directive | Patient's written wishes for care if they can't speak | Ask case management if the chart has none |
| MAR | Medication administration record | The list you scan/dot against; right patient, right time, right everything |
| CBC, BMP | Complete blood count; basic metabolic panel — everyday lab panels | You don't need to memorize normals week one — keep a reference card |
| Restraint order | Facility policy governs; requires orders, monitoring, documentation | Never applied freely; follow the policy exactly |
| Isolation category | Rooms with added precautions (e.g., droplet, contact) | Check the sign before entering; PPE per policy |

## Shift-shape words

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Assignment / ratio | Which patients are yours; how many depends on unit and state law |
| Acuity | How sick / complex a patient is; drives staffing math |
| Sitter | A person assigned to watch a patient the whole shift (safety) |
| Rescue / balloon pump / drips | Words that mean "this patient needs a higher-acuity nurse" — get help, don't go alone |
| Oncoming shift report | The same handoff, in the other direction — your sheet is the source |

## Escalation phrases worth memorizing

When you need something done, these four deliver:

1. "I'm not comfortable with this — can you come look?" (a completely acceptable sentence)
2. "This concern is new in the last hour and it's trending the wrong way."
3. "I need a second opinion on this interpretation."
4. "Let's do a read-back to be sure we agree."

## The reminder that matters most

Nobody expects you to know all of this yet. Everyone expects you to **look it up** and **ask**. The words above are enough to make you dangerous, in the safe direction.
