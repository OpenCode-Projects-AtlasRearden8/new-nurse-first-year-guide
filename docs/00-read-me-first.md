# Read This First

**Education only — this guide is never medical advice.** This manual teaches habits, checklists, and self-management ideas for new nurses in their first year. It does not teach clinical care, does not override your employer's policies, your state's practice acts, your preceptor, or your provider team. If a situation touches patient safety, follow your facility's protocols and escalate per policy — that comes before anything written here.

## Who this is for

- Any licensed nurse in year one — med-surg, step-down, post-op, telemetry, or whatever floor you landed on.
- Nurses who are capable and still feel like fakes. That feeling is data about your stage, not about your worth.
- Anyone who wants the first year to feel survivable instead of just endurable.

It was written in plain English, step by step, the way a friend would explain it — never condescending, no jargon without a translation.

## How to use it

1. Skim everything once early in orientation so nothing feels like a surprise.
2. Use the worksheets weekly, not perfectly.
3. Reread the burnout chapter whenever your first-year weather turns.
4. Borrow one habit at a time. Stacking too many new systems is how they all collapse.

## The one-line summary

Nobody feels ready on day one. Ready is built at roughly 1% per shift, and around shift 100 it starts to feel like comfort. Your job in year one is not to be fast — it is to be safe, curious, and still standing.
